# Event and effect survey for ADR 0004

**Snapshot:** `c0b5806ce2bbc23142d4cdb5ffe006bbc78a391c` on 2026-10-06. This is a read-only survey of `deadvox/src` at that commit, input to ADR 0004 design pass 2, not a design or proposed API. The code will drift. Event and effect counts below are rough groupings, not counts of individual call sites. No code was changed for this survey.

The survey found roughly seventeen event-candidate groups and thirteen effect-vocabulary groups. Four boundaries are particularly uncertain: which game facts should cross into mod rules, how much state rules need to read, whether sound playback belongs in or follows an effect, and whether spawn/loot generation is authored rule or engine work. The current code has few general event handoffs: `core/sim.ts`, `Simulation`, types simulation facts such as damage, death, interruption, sound, and admitted player noise; much gameplay behavior instead calls its state owner directly.

Paths below are relative to `deadvox/`. “Core-mod candidate” means the behavior looks like a rule that content could own; it is not a recommendation to move it. Engine includes hot-path, exactness-critical, persistence, presentation, and lifecycle work.

## Event candidates

### Simulation cadence and player activity

- **Scheduler ticks — engine.** `src/core/scheduler.ts`, `Scheduler.advance` and `nextDue`, selects due systems in simulation-time order, with registration order breaking ties. `src/core/sim.ts`, `Simulation` registers needs and long-action work; `src/game/session.ts`, `createSession`, registers zombies, player physics, handling, firearms and lights. Frequencies are their declared simulation rates: player/firearm work is physics cadence, zombie/handling work uses a slower cadence, and needs, lights and long actions use bounded slow-system steps under compression. These are continuous engine tasks, not useful one-event-per-tick mod notifications by default. They read scheduler cursors, compression, player/session state and the state owned by each system. Whether any should become a coarse mod-facing “time advanced” fact is uncertain.
- **Player movement and actions — engine trigger, possible rule facts.** `src/game/session.ts`, the player system's scheduler callback and `dispatchPlayerActions`, sample movement, sprint, jump and hand-use intents at player ticks. `src/game/play.ts`, `performHandUse`, routes selected item actions; `playKeys`, `use`, and `useTarget` route interaction, rest, reading, furniture and cancellation. Movement reads controls, body, inventory burden, stamina and handling state; actions read selected items, target, reach, content definitions and current action state. Movement/collision and input timing are engine-owned; named coarse facts such as an item being used or a furniture interaction completing might be rule candidates, but are not emitted as general events today. Frequency ranges from each physics tick to a single input/action.

### Needs, injury, action completion and progression

- **Needs and health — mixed; candidate rule facts.** `src/core/sim.ts`, the needs system and `Simulation.hurt`, advance needs at the registered cadence and admit damage. `src/core/needs.ts`, `stepNeeds` and `consume`, update calories, hydration, fatigue, health and stamina or report need-related interruptions. `game/survival.ts`, `finishEating`, consumes food and applies food-poisoning injury. Need changes occur on needs ticks or a completed eating action; damage occurs on a hit or poisoning. They read current needs, game-time conversion, rest rate, god mode, food state and health. `Simulation` owns needs and death; the engine should retain exact advancement and damage application. Need thresholds, consequences and poisoning rules look like core-mod candidates. Damage, interruption and death are already `SimEvent` facts; ordinary need deltas are not.
- **Melee hits and firearm hits — engine resolution; rule consequences possible.** `src/core/playerCombat.ts`, `PlayerCombat.tick` resolves a swing once at its contact point after validating the held hands. `src/core/zombies.ts`, `ZombieSystem.swing`, resolves the ray hit, damage, hit region, knockback, severing, incapacitation or death; `firePellets` resolves pellets. `src/game/firearmHandling.ts`, `FirearmMechanics.fire` and `src/game/session.ts`, the firearm callbacks, commit firearm state, aim/recoil, zombie hits, ejection and sound. These fire per swing contact or committed shot, not per frame; each reads aim/contact geometry, weapon/firearm definition and state, target state, condition and relevant skill. Ray tests, contact timing, collision, pellet geometry and exact hit application are engine territory. Damage type, weapon consequences, wound/status rules, and coarse “hit” or “target incapacitated” facts could support rules. Current zombie effect callbacks are notifications to presentation/session owners, not a general mod rule bus.
- **Long actions finishing or stopping — split by action kind.** `src/core/longAction.ts`, `LongActions.advance` and `finishJob`, advances rest/sleep, craft and reading under compressed simulation time, validates owners, and invokes completion once. `src/game/rest.ts`, `RestController`, checks furniture and reach; `src/core/craftWork.ts`, `craftActionHooks`, owns craft progression and completion; `src/core/bookReading.ts`, `bookReadingHooks`, owns reading completion. Start, resume, stop, interruption and finish happen per player request, scheduler step or external interruption. They read action progress, needs, compression, inventory/work, character, furniture, reach and book/recipe definitions. Exact progress, cancellation, resumability and save state are engine-owned; craft/reading/recovery outcomes and skill/knowledge grants are rule candidates. These completions currently have no general event record.
- **Practice and learned knowledge — core-mod candidates.** `src/core/craftWork.ts`, `craftActionHooks.finish`, awards practice for the completed recipe; `src/core/bookReading.ts`, `bookReadingHooks.finish`, teaches declared recipes; `src/core/character.ts`, `Character.awardPractice` and `learnRecipes`, own the resulting progression. They occur once per successful completion and read recipe skill/time or book recipe declarations plus current character state. Character owns skill levels and known recipes; the engine applies progression exactly. Whether individual practice/recipe-learning outcomes should themselves be emitted is open.
- **Rest, sleep and interruption — mixed.** `src/core/longAction.ts`, `LongActions.startRest`, `advance`, and `syncInterruption`, begin/advance/finish or suspend a rest action; sleep interruption wakes the player and clears the sleep action. `src/game/rest.ts`, `RestController`, validates nearby furniture and selects a rate. `src/core/sim.ts`, `Simulation.checkInterruptions` (called by `frame`) consumes interrupt facts and halts compression. Start/stop are per action; progress follows long-action ticks; interruption checks occur around frame advances and after system ticks. They read furniture, reach, fatigue, compression and `SimEvent` interrupts. The engine owns scheduling, compressed time and cancellation semantics; rest quality, sleep benefits and interruption consequences are rule candidates. A general event for rest start/finish/wake is absent.
- **Time of day — coarse rule input candidate, exact clock engine-owned.** `src/core/clock.ts`, calendar conversion helpers, and `Simulation.calendar` derive calendar time; `src/game/session.ts`, `playerSense` and the zombie system's hour callback, feed time into zombie perception/behavior. It is read whenever the relevant systems tick, rather than emitted as a transition event. The clock and conversion are engine/exactness work; a coarse time-period fact may be useful to rules. Day/night transitions are not currently first-class events.

### Sound, perception and zombie lifecycle

- **Sound admission and playback — uncertain boundary.** `src/game/session.ts`, `admitSound`, `playWorldSound` and `playPlayerSound`, ask `src/core/soundPicker.ts`, `SoundPicker.pick`, whether authored sound can be admitted. An admitted sound emits `SimEvent` sound; player sounds whose definition enables noise also update session-owned vocal-noise state and emit a `noise` event. This runs per sound occurrence (footstep and some AI sounds can be frequent; actions such as doors and shots are discrete). It reads sound definitions, picker cooldown/RNG state, source and position, and player/noise metadata. Sound choice/cooldown and simulation hearing are behaviorally significant; audio decoding, mixing and device output are engine-owned. Whether mod rules select sounds, emit sound intents, or react to admitted sounds is uncertain. The split is visible: `game/audio.ts` plays output after session admission.
- **Noise heard and perception changes — engine sensing, possible coarse rule facts.** `src/core/zombies.ts`, `hearPlayer`, `hearVocalNoise`, `perceivePlayer`, `ZombieSystem.updateAttention` and `checkInvestigationArrival`, evaluate sound/noise, sight, light and target arrival while zombie AI ticks (there is no smell sense at this snapshot; it is deferred to #278). They update target, mode, investigation/chase and related per-zombie state; alert/idle sounds are emitted through callbacks. They read player body/movement/light/vocal noise, sound radius and lifetime, geometry/occlusion, time and zombie state. Perception, ray/occlusion tests, targeting cadence and AI state transitions are engine candidates because of hot-path and exactness concerns. “Heard noise,” “noticed player,” or “lost target” are possible coarse facts for rules; none is currently handed to a general event consumer. Frequency is per active zombie AI update, with player noise refreshed only when an admitted sound occurs.
- **Zombie attack, incapacitation, death and severing — mixed.** `src/core/zombies.ts`, `ZombieSystem.resolveZombieAttack`, `swing`, and `firePellets`, process attacks/hits and callback outcomes. `src/game/session.ts`, `createSession`'s `hurtPlayer`, `onSevered` and `onDeath` callbacks, apply player wear/damage, create severed parts, roll loot and drop it. Attack checks run at zombie ticks; player attacks resolve at melee contact or pellet impact; death effects occur once on the outcome. They read health/limbs/armor, weapon, target and world positions, inventory, zombie type and loot definitions. Physics, hit resolution and AI are engine-owned; attack eligibility, wounds, loot rules and ability consequences are core-mod candidates. Death and player damage facts exist in `SimEvent`; zombie-specific combat facts do not.
- **Column loading, spawning and loot generation — lifecycle and exactness-critical.** `src/game/session.ts`, `onColumn`, populates site furniture; `src/core/zombieSpawns.ts`, `ZombieSpawner.onColumn`, adds deterministic authored spawns once per spawn key; `src/core/site.ts` and `src/core/loot.ts`, `rollLoot`, generate site loot; `createSession`'s zombie `onDeath` rolls death loot. Column arrival is per loaded column; each spawn/loot roll is per authored placement or death. They read site generation, seed/RNG, content tables, spawn ledger and world position. Chunk loading, deterministic RNG, anti-respawn state and save continuation are engine-owned. Loot tables and spawn declarations are rule/content candidates; whether mods need a “column loaded” event or should only author data remains uncertain.

### Furniture, items, persistence and presentation

- **Doors, locks, searches and furniture — core-mod consequences over engine admission.** `src/game/doorAction.ts`, `registerDoorAction`, applies queued door open/close/lock/unlock; `src/core/blockEntities.ts`, `BlockEntities.setOpen`, `closeDoor`, `setLocked` and `markSearched`, own furniture state. `src/game/session.ts`, search action handler and `search`, queue a search and mark it searched only on completion/reach. These happen once per completed action, with close also checking player/zombie blockers; they read furniture definition/state, keys, reach and bodies. Collision, occupancy and block-entity indexing are engine-owned; lock/access rules, search rewards and door sound choices could be rules. There is no general door-opened, locked or searched event.
- **Short item actions and handling — mixed.** `src/core/handling.ts`, `HandlingQueue.tick`, completes queued item moves/actions and reports outcomes; its action handlers live with their owners. `src/game/survival.ts`, registered eating/battery actions and `Survival.use`, consume food, swap batteries, change lights or begin reading. `src/game/play.ts`, `performHandUse` and quickbar handlers, routes primary item actions. Each action starts from input and commits once at completion (light toggles can be immediate). These read item identity/location/definition, reach, needs and current queue state. Transfer correctness and action timing are engine-owned; item-use consequences and consumed-item outputs are rule candidates. No general event represents “item used” or “handling completed.”
- **World changes and construction — currently direct owner calls.** `src/core/world.ts`, `World` mutation methods and `restoreDiffs`, own block state; `src/core/inventory.ts`, item/work completion methods, own item changes; `src/core/blockEntities.ts`, furniture mutations, own entity state. Construction/crafting and other interactions call these owners directly, at action completion. They read recipe/plan, material, reach, current world and entity state. Grid writes, indexing, validation and save diffs are engine/exactness work; “built,” “repaired,” “deconstructed,” or “block changed” are potential coarse facts. The event comment in `src/core/events.ts` mentions block changes, which the current `SimEvent` union does not include.
- **Save and load — engine boundary, not a gameplay rule trigger.** `src/core/saveState.ts`, snapshot functions, `src/core/saveFormat.ts`, serialization/validation, `src/game/session.ts`, `snapshot` and restore setup in `createSession`, capture and rehydrate simulation, scheduler, player, inventory, world, zombies and long actions. This occurs at explicit save/load and restore operations. It reads/writes the full state owned by each subsystem and requires exact identity/validation; it is engine/persistence work, not a mod event/effect candidate. Restore reconstructs owners and callbacks rather than replaying gameplay effects.
- **Rendering and audio output — engine-only observers.** `src/game/play.ts`, frame loop and visual update, and `src/game/audio.ts`, present already-decided world state and admitted sounds. They run per rendered frame or admitted sound. They read state/events but should not own simulation outcomes. Damage display and zombie-effect callbacks are currently presentation notifications, not mod effects.

## Effect vocabulary already present

- **Damage and death:** `Simulation.hurt` applies player health loss and emits damage/interrupt/death facts; `ZombieSystem` changes zombie health/parts/incapacitation/death and calls outcome hooks. State owners are `Simulation` and `ZombieSystem`; injury severity/consequences are core-mod candidates, while exact application and death lifecycle are engine-owned.
- **Needs and stamina:** `stepNeeds`, `consume`, `stepStamina` and light/sleep systems change calories, hydration, fatigue, health and stamina. `Simulation` owns needs; the player scheduler owns stamina stepping. Survival rates and thresholds could be rule-owned; numerical progression and scheduler are engine responsibilities.
- **Skill practice and known recipes:** `Character.awardPractice` and `learnRecipes` update `Character` progression after craft/reading completion. The character remains the state owner; practice/learning grants are natural rule effects.
- **Items:** `Inventory` and `HandlingQueue` create, consume, move, equip, store and wear items; `Survival` consumes food and swaps batteries; craft/disassembly completion releases inputs and creates outputs; firearm and zombie effects create casings, severed parts and loot. Inventory owns item identity/tree/condition and transfers. A mod-facing effect should describe intent rather than mutate inventory directly; actual item creation/ownership is exact engine work.
- **Craft and repair:** inventory work state advances, consumes escrowed inputs at completion, produces recipe results or disassembly yields, and can change a repair target. `Inventory` owns work items and product state; `LongActions` owns action lifecycle; `Character` owns practice. Recipe outcomes are candidates for data/rules; escrow, reach validation and atomic completion are engine-owned.
- **Reading and rest:** reading completion adds known recipes; rest/sleep changes fatigue through needs rates and completion/interrupt notices. `LongActions` and `Simulation` own action/time state; `Character` owns learned knowledge. Learning/rest benefits can be rule outcomes; resume/sleep wake semantics are engine contracts.
- **Lights, batteries and burn state:** `Survival` changes item on/off, charge, ignition and selected light; its slow tick burns charge, extinguishes lights and emits interruption. `Inventory` owns item fields; `Survival` owns selected-light state. Fuel/burn consequences might be data-driven, but exact clock charge and item consistency are engine work.
- **Furniture and world:** `BlockEntities` changes open/locked/searched state; `World` owns block changes/diffs; inventory/work completion handles construction material and outputs. Door policy and construction outcomes may be rule effects; spatial validation, collision, state indices and persistence remain engine-owned.
- **Zombie state and spawns:** `ZombieSystem` owns health, behavior, awareness, movement, attacks, incapacitation/death; `ZombieSpawner` owns spawn de-duplication. Ability consequences, evolution and spawn rules are possible core-mod vocabulary. AI, movement, perception queries and lifecycle integrity are engine work.
- **Noise and sound:** `createSession` owns vocal-noise identity/lifetime and the sound picker; `Simulation.events` carries admitted sound/noise; `GameAudio` owns output playback. Noise as a world stimulus may be an effect; audio playback is not a deterministic game-state effect. Whether sound choice is effect data or engine-resolved policy is uncertain.
- **Interruptions and notices:** `Simulation` owns compression interruption and pending interrupt state; long actions own stopped/completed state. Player-facing notices are presentation. Rules may cause a coarse interruption intent, but the engine must decide how it interacts with active actions.
- **Aim, firearm cycles and melee action:** `AimController`, `FirearmMechanics` and `PlayerCombat` own recoil/aim, firearm chamber/cycle and melee timing/contact state. Weapon rules and consequences may be data-driven; exact ballistics, timing and animation-facing action state are engine-owned.
- **Save state:** each subsystem owns its snapshot/restore representation, joined by `snapshotSession` and validated by save-format code. This is not a rule effect; any new persistent mod-owned state would require an explicit save boundary.

Existing first-class simulation event variants are limited to interruption, player damage, player death, admitted sound and player noise in `src/core/sim.ts`, `SimEvent`. Many other changes above are direct calls or callbacks. A callback's existence does not by itself make it a rule event: e.g. zombie sever/death callbacks serve session drops and presentation.

## Mind Over Matter gaps

Mind Over Matter suggests a mod may need to observe character-stat and skill changes, action/ability activation, hits and injuries, status changes, time and environmental conditions, and to produce persistent character state, statuses, ability/cooldown changes, item transfers/creation and world consequences. Deadvox has skills and learned recipes, needs, item conditions, firearm cycles, noise, zombie state and long actions, but not equivalents for several general-purpose concepts:

- No general character-stat/attribute vocabulary or stat-change event/effect; `Character` currently owns progression skills and recipes, while `Simulation` owns survival needs.
- No generalized status-effect/trait owner with add/remove/stack/tick semantics, nor an event for status applied, expired or resisted.
- No spell/power/ability activation and no persistent per-character ability list, level, cooldown or resource protocol. Firearms and lights are specialized item mechanics, not generic abilities.
- No general event vocabulary for successful/failed item use, craft/repair completion, hit target/location/type, zombie perception transition, furniture/world change, or time-period transition. The internal facts exist in local owners but are not consistently exposed.
- No mod-owned persistent state lifecycle or save namespace. Current save code snapshots known engine owners and requires exact format/version identity.

These are benchmark gaps, not a prescribed API. In particular, the survey does not settle how rules read state or which mechanics stay in the engine.

## Unclassified or out of scope

The source scan groups behavior by owning subsystem; it does not enumerate each content definition, UI action, rendering callback, status-like item field, or every direct mutation. It does not inspect external mods beyond the Mind Over Matter benchmark already cited by ADR 0004. It does not determine which events are useful to mod authors, whether rule state should be copied, queried or supplied as a view, how competing effects resolve, or what migration steps should be. Exact event/effect grouping and the engine/mod classification remain provisional; the four uncertainties in the summary need pass-2 design rather than inference from current call graphs.
