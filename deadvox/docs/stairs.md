# Explicit storeys, stairs and root cellars

Curated pieces own stair geometry and overhead openings, just as they own walls and floors. Access metadata describes traversal rather than permission to overwrite another owner. An owned piece cannot use a generated flight post-pass; author the transition within its delegated space. See `src/core/templates.ts`, `compileTemplate`.

Templates awaiting the scoped migration in #712 retain their named-flight compiler. That exception is not permission to generate architecture for new curated pieces. See `src/core/stairFlight.ts`, `planFlight` and `constructFlight`, and `../../docs/jsonnet.md`, "Curated map features".

Template coordinates are blocks; site placement remains in metres. Storey height is standing-feet height, while layer indices identify support layers. Placement lowers the template origin for below-ground floors. Air cells in the authored template carve the cellar consistently, including across chunk borders; the surrounding terrain and structure remain. See `src/core/templates.ts`, `compileTemplate`, and `src/core/authoredSite.ts`, `AuthoredSite.stamp`.

Validation checks standing clearance and reachable floor space through openings, doors and stairs. This is a construction check, not runtime pathfinding. Doors remain openable for validation, while other solid furniture blocks a route. An upper-floor opening must stay inside the incoming flight's footprint and headroom, or it leaves a gap at the top of the stairs. See `src/core/templateSpatial.ts`, `upperFloorOpeningIssues`.

Buried rooms need world-derived sky visibility because wide ambient occlusion retains a nonzero ambient floor. Cellars share the site-structure discovery path with above-ground rooms; a below-ground template flag is not a lighting selector. Fields remain local to their geometry rather than spanning empty gaps. Conservative readiness and limited residency can darken a distant room, so the witness must prove both local darkness and correct field selection. This is bounded diffuse transport, not photometry. A material comparison needs current published fields, otherwise successive screenshots can mistake discovery or revalidation for a material or ambient-occlusion defect. Disable animated film during independent lighting captures: different grain realizations are not a sky-visibility difference. Keep the other post-processing effects so the comparison still exercises the played lighting path. Arrival observations, geometry settlement and pixel comparisons keep their full viewport. Initial mesh readiness remains independent of lighting completion. See `src/core/skyTransport.ts`, `buildSkyResult`, and `test/browser/stairs.mjs`, `lightingCellarProofs`.

Shamblers do not navigate stairs as routes. A resident can drift onto a flight while beelining, but a target on another floor does not grant stair knowledge. Door bashing remains deferred under #273 until mob and obstacle strength exist. See `src/core/zombies.ts`, `ZombieSystem.tick`.

The first-mesh reference keeps main's presentation and startup dependencies coherent while retaining branch core contracts, fixture content and browser. Adapt reference callers at that boundary instead of reverting the branch's core owner: interaction picking needs both physical reach and visual access even when main supplies a single predicate. A caller mismatch can halt startup before geometry arrives, and the loading owner can catch it without a page error. The reference measures delivered geometry, not first rendered pixels or a pristine whole build; observations are not default timing assertions. Enter Play before measuring because the title screen does not construct the played world. Missing overlays and browser errors invalidate the evidence. Use the exact event base because shallow merge parents need not contain it. See `test/browser/sky-main-arrival.mjs`, `mainSourceOverlays`, `adaptMainInteractionPick` and `measureMainFirstMesh`; `src/game/worldLoading.ts`, `WorldLoading.fail`; and `test/sky-main-arrival.test.mjs`.

The performance gate needs a real GPU on the immutable published preview. Software-rendered CI establishes correctness, ownership and admission fairness, not a frame-rate verdict. Its relative job trace remains useful for locating candidate selection, snapshot preparation, admission, execution and publication delay; dispatch and return gaps also contain delivery and scheduling, not pure transfer. See `test/browser/sky-timing.mjs`, `summarizeSkyTiming`.

The debug route flag drives the controlled camp through the input and noclip owners, not direct pose writes or a second simulation loop. Forwarded holds retain native modifier state and release only their own shared keys, so reversing direction cannot cancel a still-owned sprint hold. See `src/game/inputBindings.ts`, `forwardedActionHolds`. Its launch link owns the complete diagnostic preset; offering the flag independently in the public launcher would mix uncontrolled scenarios into the hardware gate. Launch through `src/debug/skyRoute.ts`, `skyRouteUrl`, click Play and keep the mouse still. A wrong start, interruption or motion-budget exit produces partial evidence rather than completion. The console and overlay share a copyable JSON snapshot with renderer identity, actual dimensions, frame/job denominators and resolved versus censored camera-fallback and revalidation episodes. Outstanding jobs remain censored even if replies arrive after the route boundary. Empty denominators mean unavailable evidence, not success. A route rejected before motion has no geometry evidence: probing an unrelated scene adds work without earning useful-work coverage. See `src/debug/skyRouteMetrics.ts`, `skyRouteSummary`.

Lighting measurements cover CPU service plus owned texture, uniform and program submission. Attribution begins at route activation: combining earlier startup or menu rendering into its first frame would invent a multi-frame service spike. Startup jobs, initial state and censoring evidence remain in the report; discarding them would hide work merely because it preceded activation. See `src/debug/skyRoute.ts`, `attachSkyRoute`. GPU lighting execution is unavailable, never a zero-cost claim. Geometry probes run after measurement and use the uninstrumented sampler so they cannot improve its service numbers. The owned selector/shader witness runs before entering Play, so its awaited worker steps cannot contend with the unrelated full-size game render loop. See `test/browser/sky-selection.mjs`, `skySelectionProof`. The observer must preserve the frame handler's continuation result even when nothing draws; it does not own scheduling. See `src/debug/skyRouteObserver.ts`, `observeSkyRoute`, and `src/game/frameDriver.ts`, `observeRealFrame`. BR owns the hardware gate judgment; neither these observations nor default tests declare acceptance.

The stairs site is a demonstration fixture, not the final Beat 1 house or Dad's Beat 3 cabin. Browser traversal proves player physics with noclip off; lighting checks are separate pixel witnesses. A screenshot alone does not prove floor support. Ladders and roof hatches remain a separate design in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).
